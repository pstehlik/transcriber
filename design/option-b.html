So I think the most interesting thing about working with local speech-to-text models is just how far they've come in the last couple of years. When we first started experimenting with Whisper back in 2022, the accuracy was impressive but the speed on consumer hardware was really prohibitive. Now with MLX optimizations on Apple Silicon, we're seeing near real-time transcription on a MacBook Pro. The medium model hits a great sweet spot between accuracy and performance. You can process a five-minute audio clip in about forty seconds. One thing I'd recommend is always normalizing your audio input to 16 kilohertz mono before feeding it to the model. The model will do this internally anyway, but you get more predictable results if you handle the resampling yourself with a proper audio library. The output formats are flexible too. We typically use plain text for quick transcriptions, but the JSON format gives you word-level timestamps which are invaluable for building things like subtitle generators or searchable audio archives. I think the next frontier is really going to be speaker diarization combined with transcription. Being able to automatically identify who is speaking and what they're saying opens up a whole new class of applications, from meeting summarizers to podcast indexing tools.
